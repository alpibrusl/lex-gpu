# P2: Metal, correct

## Goal

Llama-3-8B in int4 producing correct tokens on the Mac, at any speed: the full
op set in MSL, int4 dequantisation, paged KV, safetensors loading, sampling.
No fusion, no tuning; those are P3.

## Status

**The exit test is met.** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in int4 (Q4_K_M), exactly
as Ollama serves it, runs on lex kernels on the M4 Max. Its greedy tokens
are identical to Ollama's.

The two models tested, each greedy-decoded on 4 prompts × 24 tokens next to
Ollama itself:

| Model (Ollama tag) | Weights | Tokens identical to Ollama | Worst top-5 log-prob gap vs Ollama | vs f32 reference | lex tok/s | Ollama tok/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `llama3.2:1b` | Q8_0 | 96 / 96 | 0.0076 | 0.0063 | 13 | ~275 |
| `llama3.1:8b` | Q4_K_M (Q4_K + Q6_K) | 96 / 96 | 0.090 | 0.0066 | 6 | ~86 |

Every layer op is a typed `lex-front` program, lowered to MSL:
- RMSNorm;
- quantised matrix-vector, with a fused residual;
- RoPE;
- flash-decode attention over an f16 KV cache;
- SiLU·mul;
- the output head.

Quantised weights of any supported format are repacked at load into `I8`
values plus per-group f32 scales (and mins, for Q4_K), and meet them through
the checked `dequant` op. For Q4_K and Q6_K the repacking is **bit-identical**
to ggml's reference dequantisation (unit-tested against a line-by-line
transcription).

## The Ollama test

It uses two oracles and three comparisons:

```text
   Ollama (llama.cpp)  <-- llama_ref.py -->  PyTorch reference  <-- cargo test --> lex on the GPU
          ^                                                                               |
          +------------------------------ lex_vs_ollama.py -----------------------------+
```

- **`scripts/llama_ref.py`:** reads the GGUF blob Ollama serves (`gguf.py`
  dequantises it) and runs a float32 Llama in PyTorch. It asks Ollama for the
  same greedy continuation with top-5 log-probabilities. Every token is
  identical: 3 prompts × 16 steps on the 1B, and 3 × 12 on the 8B. The worst
  differences are 0.0068 (1B) and 0.057 (8B). It then writes what the
  reference computed to `crates/lex-rt/tests/data/<model>_golden.txt`.
- **`cargo test -p lex-rt --test llama_ollama`:** runs those prompts through
  lex, one test per model. Every greedy token matches, and every top-5
  log-probability is within 0.0063 (1B) and 0.0066 (8B) of the reference. The
  8B test takes 12 s and peaks at 20 GB. It needs the models locally; CI has
  none, so there it skips with a message.
- **`scripts/lex_vs_ollama.py`:** lex against Ollama directly, on any
  prompt. The default 4 prompts give 96/96 identical tokens on both models.
  The worst differences are 0.0076 (1B) and 0.090 (8B).

**Why the tolerance is not zero.** Two reasons:
- llama.cpp's quantised Metal kernels round differently from an f32
  computation. The gap grows with depth and with the 4-bit formats: see the
  8B numbers below.
- lex keeps K/V in f16, as llama.cpp does; the reference keeps them in f32.

**lex is closer to the exact answer than Ollama is.** On the 8B, lex and
the f32 reference agree within 0.0066. Ollama differs from both by up to
0.09, so the gap is llama.cpp's rounding, not a tile error. That is also why
the scripts' tolerance is 0.1, not 0.05. A real bug is far larger: the
missing-BOS mistake below was 1.9.

**Speed.** lex decodes at 13 tok/s on the 1B and 6 on the 8B. Ollama does
about 275 and 86. Each token is 13 dispatches per layer plus 2 (210 on the
1B, 418 on the 8B), and each one waits for the previous.
On top of that come two host round trips per layer, and matvecs that stage
every weight through threadgroup scratch. That's P2 as specified, and the P3
baseline.

## How the lowering works

It is the simplest scheme that is right for every program the checker
accepts:

- **One threadgroup per grid instance**, with `T` threads.
- **Register tiles are distributed cyclically.** Thread `t` owns flat elements
  `t, t+T, …`. Elementwise ops between equal-sized tiles never leave the
  thread.
- **Operands another thread owns** (a matmul input, a row-broadcast vector,
  a row reduction's input) are **staged** through threadgroup scratch.
- **Threadgroup tiles** get a fixed arena offset per allocation site. They
  move through copies, waits and loop carries as *pointers*, so a rotated
  double buffer is a pointer swap.
- **`copy_async` becomes a cooperative copy at issue, and `wait` a barrier.**
  Metal has no copy engine. This is sound only because linearity proves
  nobody still owns the destination.
- **Barriers are conservative:** one before every threadgroup write, and one
  between staging and use.

## Results

Flash decode on the GPU matches PyTorch SDPA within 1e-4. It also matches the
interpreter with 5 heads on the grid; only `exp` differs, in the last bits.

The speed is the baseline, not the claim. Llama-3-8B decode shape (8 KV heads
× 4 query heads, head dim 128, f16), measured on an M4 Max:

| batch × seq | lex GB/s | % of copy | PyTorch SDPA, same layout | lex / torch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 × 4096 | 23.4 | 5% | 379.5 | 0.06× |
| 16 × 8192 | 90.3 | 19% | 435.0 | 0.21× |

The kernel launches one threadgroup per (sequence, KV head), so batch 4 has 32
threadgroups on a 40-core GPU. Each one streams its whole K/V sequence
serially, behind 34 barrier sites and scalar dot products. P3's work is visible
in that sentence:
- split the sequence across threadgroups (flash-decoding);
- use simdgroup reductions and matrices instead of scratch staging;
- derive the barrier set from the effect graph.

(PyTorch's `enable_gqa=True` path on the 32-head layout reaches only
12.5 GB/s, which would make lex look 1.9× faster. That comparison is
misleading, so the script labels it and never uses it as the headline.)

## Findings

**The rotated double buffer broke the first lowering.** After `wait`, K
*aliases* its carry's buffer, so the loop's yields are a permutation of the
carries' storage. Assigning them one at a time read a pointer it had just
overwritten. Every head computed garbage, and no type error could see it: the
program was right; the lowering of its SSA was not. Carries are now assigned
in parallel through temporaries, and a register permutation is refused rather
than miscompiled.

**The checker's budget is not the backend's budget.** The Metal schedule that
fills 32 KiB exactly type-checks, but the lowering needs staging scratch on
top, so it doesn't fit. The backend reports "N B of tiles + M B of scratch"
instead of failing at pipeline creation. The checker cannot know M yet. Once
layouts are in the type, staging becomes explicit and the budget becomes one
number again.

**A token-only comparison would have hidden a real bug.** Raw mode in Ollama
prepends BOS. Without BOS, the reference still produced the same greedy tokens
as Ollama, but its log-probabilities were off by up to 1.9. Comparing
log-probabilities, not just text, is what caught it.

**Every quantisation format reduces to one op.** Q8_0 (scale), Q6_K (scale
per 16) and Q4_K (scale and min per 32) all repack into `I8` values plus
per-group f32 scales and optional mins. One `dequant` op covers them, and one
generic matvec kernel is compiled per (shape, layout). Every scale and min is a
product that is exact in f32, so the kernels see bit-for-bit the values ggml
does.

**Quantised values are a type, not a convention.** Weights are loaded as an
`I8` tensor plus f32 scales. The checker rejects any arithmetic on `I8`
except `dequant`, which pairs values with their scales and checks the group
shape. Forgetting a scale is a type error, not a subtly wrong model.

**Fast math and online softmax do not mix.** The running max starts at
`-inf`, and Metal's default fast math may assume infinities away. Typed
kernels compile with fast math off. P0's kernels keep it on, so their numbers
don't move.
